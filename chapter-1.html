My First Book

Chapter 1

The Girl Who Dreamed

Long before I knew what my life would become, I was a girl who dreamed.

I grew up in Lima, Peru, a city of contrasts, light, movement and endless possibilities. Like many children, I did not yet understand how complicated life could be. I simply looked at the world with curiosity.

I dreamed without asking whether my dreams were realistic.

I dreamed because dreaming came naturally.

As a child, I could not have imagined the roads I would one day travel, the countries I would call home, the people I would meet, the challenges I would face, or the moments that would change me forever.

But perhaps our deepest dreams begin before we have the words to explain them.

There was always something inside me that wanted to discover more.

I wanted to learn.

I wanted to understand how things worked.

I wanted to become independent.

And somewhere deep inside, I sensed that my life would take me beyond the familiar world around me.

At that age, I did not know what "a new beginning" meant.

I only knew that life was bigger than what I could see.

The First Lessons

Looking back, I realize that childhood teaches us lessons without announcing them.

We learn what love feels like.

We learn what safety means.

We learn how to trust.

We learn how to dream.

And sometimes, we also learn that life does not always follow the plans we make for it.

My family, my surroundings and my experiences shaped the person I was becoming. Some lessons came through joy. Others came through uncertainty.

But all of them became part of my story.

I believe that every person carries an invisible collection of moments from childhood — moments that may seem small at the time but remain somewhere inside us.

A conversation.

A place.

A smell.

A photograph.

A dream.

A feeling.

Years later, those fragments can return and remind us where we came from.

They can also remind us who we were before life became complicated.

A World Beyond the Horizon

As I grew older, my dreams became more defined.

Education became important to me.

I wanted to understand the world, not simply accept it as it was.

I was fascinated by technology and by the possibilities of knowledge. The idea that something could be created from an idea fascinated me.

A thought could become a plan.

A plan could become a project.

And a project could become something real.

I did not know then how important this way of thinking would become in my life.

Eventually, my path led me to Germany.

Leaving Peru and building a life in another country was not simply a geographical change. It meant entering a completely different world.

A new language.

A new culture.

A new way of working.

New expectations.

New responsibilities.

And, above all, the challenge of finding myself again in a place where almost everything was unfamiliar.

There were moments when I felt strong.

There were moments when I felt completely alone.

And there were moments when I wondered whether the dreams I had carried for so long were still possible.

But dreams have a strange quality.

Sometimes we think we have abandoned them.

Yet they remain quietly inside us.

Waiting.

What I Know Now

Today, many years later, I understand something I could not have understood as a young girl:

A dream does not have to reveal the entire path.

It only has to give you a reason to take the next step.

Life will change.

People will change.

Circumstances will change.

Even we will change.

And sometimes the greatest courage is not holding on to the life we imagined, but being willing to discover the life that is actually waiting for us.

The girl who dreamed could not see the woman I would become.

She could not know about the countries I would live in, the education I would receive, the career I would build, the people I would love, the difficult seasons I would experience, or the new dreams that would appear much later.

But she had something I never want to lose:

Hope.

Perhaps that is why I still think about her.

The little girl who believed there was something beyond the horizon.

The girl who did not yet know how hard life could become.

The girl who did not have all the answers.

But who dreamed anyway.

And perhaps that is where every new beginning starts.

Not with certainty.

Not with a perfect plan.

But with a small voice inside us whispering:

There must be more.

A THOUGHT TO CARRY WITH YOU

Never be ashamed of the dreams you had when you were young.

Some dreams may change.

Some may disappear.

Others may return years later in a completely different form.

But the ability to dream is never wasted.

Sometimes the person we were becomes the guide for the person we are becoming.
